\begin{prob*}[Munkres 52.6]
    Show that if $X$ is path connected, the 
    homomorphism induced by a continuous map 
    is independent of base point, up to 
    isomorphism of the groups involved. 
    More precisely, let $h : X \to Y$ be continuous, 
    with $h(x_0) = y_0$ and $h(x_1) = y_1$. 
    Let $\alpha$ be a path in $X$ from $x_0$ to 
    $x_1$, and let $\beta = h \circ \alpha$. Show that 
    \[
        \hat \beta \circ (h_{x_0})_* = 
        (h_{x_1})_* \circ \hat \alpha. 
    \]
    This equation expresses the fact the following 
    diagram of maps commutes. 
    \[
        \begin{tikzcd}
            \pi_1(X,x_0) \ar[d,"\hat \alpha"] \ar[r, "(h_{x_0})_*"] 
            & \pi_1(Y,y_0) \ar[d, "\hat \beta"] \\ 
            \pi_1(X,x_1) \ar[r, "(h_{x_1})_*"] 
            & \pi_1(Y,y_1) 
        \end{tikzcd}
    \]
\end{prob*}
\begin{proof}
    As $X$ is path connected, there exists 
    $\alpha : [0,1] \to X$ with 
    $\alpha(0) = x_0, \ \alpha(1) = x_1$. 

    $\alpha$ induces a change-of-base-point isomorphism 
    \[
        \hat \alpha : 
        \pi_1(X,x_0) \to \pi_1(X,x_1), 
    \]
    by 
    \[
        [f] \mapsto [\bar \alpha * f * \alpha]. 
    \]
    Similarly, $h$ induces 
    \[
        (h_{x_0})_*([f]) = [h \circ f]. 
    \]

    Let $[f] \in \pi_1(X,x_0)$. 
    Applying $(h_{x_0})_*$ gives, as mentioned,
    \[
        [f] \mapsto [h \circ f]. 
    \]
    Applying $\hat \beta$ gives 
    \[
        \hat\beta((h_{x_0})_*([f]))
        =
        [\bar\beta*(h\circ f)*\beta].
    \]
    Applying $\hat \alpha$ gives, as mentioned, 
    \[
        [f] \mapsto [\bar \alpha * f * \alpha]. 
    \]
    Applying $(h_{x_1})_*$ gives 
    \[
        (h_{x_1})_*(\hat\alpha([f]))
        =
        [h\circ(\bar\alpha*f*\alpha)].
    \]

    Now it remains to show that these expressions are equal. 

    Since $\beta = h \circ \alpha$ we have that 
    \[
        h \circ \bar \alpha = \overline{h \circ \alpha} 
        = \bar \beta. 
    \]
    For continuous maps, 
    \[
        h\circ(\gamma_1*\gamma_2)
        =
        (h\circ\gamma_1)*(h\circ\gamma_2).
    \]

    Therefore 
    \[
        \begin{aligned}
            h\circ(\bar\alpha*f*\alpha)
            &=(h\circ\bar\alpha)*(h\circ f)*(h\circ\alpha)\\
            &=\bar\beta*(h\circ f)*\beta.
        \end{aligned}
    \]
\end{proof}

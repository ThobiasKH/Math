\begin{prob*}[Munkres 23.10]
    Let $\{X_\alpha\}_{\alpha \in J}$ 
    be an indexed family of 
    connected spaces; 
    let $X$ be the product space 
    \[
        X = \prod_{\alpha \in J} X_\alpha. 
    \]

    Let $a = (a_\alpha)$ be a fixed point 
    of $X$. 

    \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
        \item Given any finite subset 
            $K$ of $J$, let $X_K$ 
            denote the subspace of $X$ 
            consisting of all 
            points $x = (x_\alpha)$ 
            such that $x_\alpha = a_\alpha$ 
            for $\alpha \not\in K$. 
            Show that $X_K$ is connected. 
        \item Show that the union $Y$
            of the spaces $X_K$ is connected. 
        \item Show that $X$ equals the 
            closure of $Y$. Conclude 
            that $X$ is connected. 
    \end{enumerate}
\end{prob*}
\begin{proof}[Proof of (a)]
    For $K \subseteq J$, finite, 
    points in $X_K$ coincide with 
    $a_\alpha$ on all but finitely many 
    $\alpha$.   
    
    Let $f : X_K \to \{0,1\}$ 
    be any continuous function. 
    We will work to show that 
    $f$ must be constant to conclude 
    that $X_K$ is connected. 

    Suppose there are two points 
    $x = (x_\alpha), \ y = (y_\alpha) \in 
    X_K$ such that $f(x) \neq f(y)$. 

    We suppose, without loss of generality, 
    that $f(x) = 0$ and $f(y) = 1$. 

    As $\{0\}, \ \{1\}$ are open in the 
    codomain, the fibers of $0$ and $1$ are 
    open sets in $X_K$ who happen to contain 
    $x$ and $y$, respectively.  

    Then, 
    \[
        f^{-1}(x) = \prod_{\alpha} 
        U_\alpha
    \]
    where finitely many $U_\alpha \neq X_\alpha$. 
    Similarly 
    \[
        f^{-1}(y) = \prod_\alpha U_\alpha 
    \]
    with finitely many $U_\alpha \neq X_\alpha$.

    Note: This is way to complicated to be 
    correct, find another way. 
\end{proof}
